Offline AI chat, free and in your browser

Most AI chats need the internet because the model lives on a server. Here the model lives on your device: it downloads once and from then on you can talk to the AI offline.

How to use the AI offline

  1. Open the chat while online and pick a model. The site recommends the one that best fits your device.
  2. Press “Download”. The model is stored in your browser (1.1 to 7.5 GB depending on the model).
  3. That’s it. Next time you can open the site with no connection: the page, the code and the model are stored in your browser.

What works offline

  • Writing and getting answers, with the same model and speed as online.
  • Your previous conversations, which are saved in your browser.
  • Settings such as the system prompt, temperature or reasoning.

You only need a connection to open the site the first time and to download a new model. When you are offline, the app shows a quiet note and keeps working.

Why it is possible

The chat uses WebGPU, the technology that lets a website use the graphics card. With it, small open language models (LFM3, Qwen3.5 and Gemma 4) run directly on your device with Transformers.js and ONNX Runtime Web. There is no AI server behind it: that is why no internet is needed, and why your messages never leave your device.

What you need

  • A browser with WebGPU: a recent Chrome or Edge on Windows, macOS or ChromeOS, Chrome on Android, or Safari 26.
  • A GPU that supports 16-bit numbers (shader-f16). The light model runs on integrated graphics.
  • Free space for the model: 1.1 GB (LFM3), 2.8 GB (Qwen3.5 4B) or 7.5 GB (Gemma 4 12B).

Frequently asked questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No sign-up, ads or subscription: your device does the computing, so there are no servers to pay for.

Can I use it on a plane or with no signal?

Yes, if you opened the site and downloaded the model beforehand. After that it works the same offline.

Is it as good as ChatGPT?

No. These are small models that fit on a personal computer: useful for writing, summarising, translating or simple coding, but they make more mistakes than large commercial models.
